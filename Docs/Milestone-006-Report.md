## Summary

Ironfront now has a consistent look: stylised near-future military hardware on an arid steppe. Every model, texture, sprite, icon and sound was generated by code in this repository. No external, purchased or AI-generated assets were used, and nothing was sent to an external service.

- **Models:**
  - procedural, faceted models for all seven requested entities plus the sandbox outpost;
  - team colour comes from livery parts, so one model serves every faction;
  - the gameplay prefabs keep their colliders, agents, components and values.
- **Environment:**
  - painted terrain with roads, base pads and ore stains;
  - a framed backdrop, so the map edge no longer shows void;
  - styled obstacles with **byte-identical NavMesh polygons**;
  - collider-free props;
  - new lighting and post-processing.
- **Effects:**
  - pooled particle effects driven only by gameplay events: muzzle flashes, tracers, impacts, explosions, debris, scorch marks;
  - burning wrecks, damage smoke and fire;
  - construction rise-in, vehicle motion and ambient animation.
- **Audio:** 33 synthesised sounds, positioned by screen location, with voice limiting, an aggregated engine loop, ambience and persistent volume settings.
- **UI:**
  - a skinned HUD with model icons, glyphs, a power meter and selection portraits;
  - **new:** main menu, pause and options menu, restyled result screen.
- **Evidence:**
  - EditMode **181/181** (162 existing plus 19 new);
  - PlayMode **49/49** (42 existing plus 7 new);
  - two complete AI-vs-AI matches;
  - release benchmark compared with the M005 baseline: +0.2–0.7 ms per frame, 333 fps at 200 units in combat.

## Phase 1 — Visual audit and art direction

To make before and after comparable, I added `Diagnostics/ShowcaseRunner` (`-ironfrontShowcase -showcaseOut <dir>`). It stages fixed shots in `RTS_Match`, with the computer commander disabled so the layout is reproducible:

It ran on a build of the untouched M005 content before any visual change, and on the final build. `Editor/Art/ArtPreview` renders model sheets in the Editor for quick iteration.

## Phase 2 — Vehicle and building models

All models come from `Editor/Art/MeshKit`, a small procedural hard-surface modeller written for this milestone:
- chamfered boxes, tapered blocks, extrusions, lathes and tubes;
- flat shading with automatically outward-facing faces;
- one sub-mesh per material.

| Entity | Silhouette cue | Animated parts |
|---|---|---|
| Light Tank | Low wedge hull, small octagonal turret, single long gun | Turret yaw (existing), recoil, tread scroll, hull tilt, dust, exhaust |
| Heavy Tank | Slab hull with skirt armour, big angular turret, twin guns | Same, heavier recoil and flash |
| Harvester | Six wheels, open hopper with a visible load, cutter drum | Drum spin, beacon, ore dust and sparkle, cargo fill (existing) |
| Headquarters | Stepped bastion, glazed tower, helipad | Rotating radar, team-coloured beacon |
| Refinery | Sawtooth hall, banded silos, glowing intake hopper | Chimney smoke |
| Power Plant | Twin hyperboloid cooling towers, team-glow coils | Steam |
| Vehicle Factory | Ribbed arched hangar, hazard door, gantry crane | Vent smoke |

Gameplay was kept separate from the visuals:
- Builders still create the same root components with the same values.
- Models are added as renderer children with no colliders.
- The named nodes that gameplay and tests rely on are preserved (`TurretPivot/Turret`, `Muzzle`, `Cargo`, `Crystals`).

EditMode tests check:
- every structure model stays inside its footprint;
- tank parts are exposed correctly;
- livery renderers are single-material;
- models add no colliders;
- polygon and renderer budgets hold.

The only gameplay-adjacent code change: `CombatUnit.TryFire` no longer calls the muzzle-flash effect directly. `WeaponFx` listens to the existing `Fired` event instead.

## Phase 3 — Environment and terrain

- **Ground:** a 1024² texture painted per scene, plus a tiled detail map. Roads, ruts, base pads, ore stains and edge falloff are paint only.
- **Backdrop:** a skirt plus boulder ridges beyond the 100 m square; visual only.
- **Obstacles:** same names, centres and box sizes; styled meshes inside each box. Verification: the rebuilt NavMeshes differ from M005 only in 4 × 16-byte tile source hashes (the colliders are now unscaled boxes of identical size); polygon data is byte-identical. `MoveOrder_SpreadsUnitsIntoFormation_AroundObstacles` and every navigation test pass.
- **Props:** no colliders. Rubble (≤ 0.35 m) at obstacle feet; shrubs and pebbles (≤ 0.42 m) in the open, never on roads, base pads or ore; barrels, crates, sandbags and tank traps only outside the playable square.
- **Lighting:** warm sun, cool ambient, light haze, an ACES post stack and SMAA. Shadow distance went from 50 m to 100 m (the camera can be 60 m away).

## Phase 4 — Combat effects and animation

`Visuals/FxLibrary` keeps one world-space particle system per effect type and emits into it, so effects create no GameObjects. It also:
- view-culls effects outside the camera;
- applies particle budgets, with movement dust and ambient smoke giving way first in big fights.

Projectiles keep the M005 `FxPool` and gain HDR tracer trails.

The components are `WeaponFx`, `VehicleMotionFx`, `DamageFx`, `DestructionFx` with `WreckBurner`, `ConstructionFx`, `StructureAnimator`, `HarvesterFx` and `OreDepositFx`. They all react to gameplay events or state and never write combat values. Wrecks have no collider and no Health; PlayMode tests confirm they are never targetable or selectable and that they remove themselves.

Placed buildings are functional immediately, as before; only the model rises (tested). Structures already present when a scene loads appear instantly.

## Phase 5 — Audio

`Core/Audio/SfxSynth` generates every sound from deterministic recipes. `Editor/Art/AudioContentBuilder` writes the WAVs and the `SoundBank`.

**Playback** (`GameAudio`):
- volume and pan follow on-screen position;
- `VoiceLimiter` caps simultaneous voices and enforces minimum intervals per sound (tested: 20 simultaneous cannon calls start 1 voice);
- one engine loop scales with the number of moving vehicles in view;
- wind ambience plus occasional distant-battle rumble.

**Volumes:** Master × Effects / Interface / Unit radio / Ambience, persisted in PlayerPrefs.

**Events** (`AudioEventRouter`, which listens to existing events; no gameplay changes):
- select, move and attack acknowledgements (radio chirps; there is no voice acting);
- placement errors, unit ready, ore delivered, under-attack alert, victory and defeat;
- UI hover and click (`UiSound`).

## Phase 6 — Interface and presentation

- **Skin:** generated chamfered 9-slice panels and buttons, amber headings, text shadows (`UiTheme`, `UiSkin`, `UiArtBuilder`).
- **Sidebar:** model-rendered icons on build and production buttons, a credits crystal glyph, a power bolt plus load meter (green / amber above 85 % / red when low).
- **Selection panel:** shows the selected type's portrait.
- **New screens:**
  - `MainMenu` scene, first in Build Settings: a diorama of the real models, Start, Options, Quit;
  - pause menu (F10 or the MENU button): Resume, Options, Restart, Main menu;
  - audio options with 5 sliders;
  - result screen: card, emblem, Restart, Main menu.
- **Preserved from M005:** the 1600×900 reference scaler, every HUD element, and every object name and label the tests use (for example, the "Credits: N" label).
- **Checked:** screenshots at 1920×1080 only.

**Reading:**
- The richer art costs **0.2–0.7 ms per frame** and **about 30–50 MB**. The worst case (200 units in combat) runs at **333 fps**, against 439 before. The p99 stays under 6 ms in every phase, and draw calls are **lower** at 200 units (647 → 530).
- **Run selection:** the table uses runs 3–5 of the final build. Runs 1 and 2 were the first launches after the long test session. Their frame times were the same (3.16 and 3.14 ms at 200-unit combat), but they showed 2–11 GC collections per phase where runs 3–5 and the baseline show 0–2. The cause is unconfirmed; it did not reproduce in three further runs. All five runs are in `Docs/Performance/M006`.
- **Isolated stalls:** single frames of 555 ms (start of 25-unit combat) and 29 ms appeared once each, in one run only. The other runs peaked at 6–11 ms in those phases. M005 saw similar one-off stalls (1073 ms). **[playtest]**: watch for hitches, especially the first battle of a session, when the particle systems are created.

**Regression found and fixed during this phase:**
1. The first M006 build ran 200-unit combat at **7.04 ms** (142 fps), with render-thread time up from 0.77 to 6.0 ms and memory growing to 512 MB.
2. Reducing particles changed nothing (7.03 ms), so I added A/B switches (`Visuals/VisualFeatures`: `-fxNoTreads`, `-fxNoTrails`, `-fxNoWrecks`, `-fxNoParticles`, `-fxNoTilt`) and measured each feature in one build:

   | Variant | Avg ms at 200-unit combat |
   |---|---|
   | All features off | 2.39 |
   | Tread scroll off | 2.68 |
   | Wrecks off | 6.73 |
   | Particles off | 6.75 |
   | Trails off | 7.06 |

3. The cause was **per-frame tread-scroll property blocks on every moving tank**. They re-upload renderer data and drop out of SRP batching.
4. The fix: one shared "moving tread" material per track material, scrolled once per frame. A tank swaps to it only when it starts or stops moving. Result: 3.0 ms, and memory flat in combat (about 385 MB, against 512 MB before the fix). The particle budgets and view culling added during the investigation were kept as cheap insurance.

## Problems encountered

- **`[RequireComponent]` ordering:** adding `OreDepositFx` before `OreDeposit` made Unity auto-add one, and the builder then added a second. Three PlayMode tests caught the doubled `OreDeposit.All` and I fixed the order.
- **Emission keyword:** `_EMISSION` was reset when new materials were imported; it is now re-applied after saving.
- **Ground masks:** `Mathf.SmoothStep` interpolates between its first two arguments rather than masking, so every road and pad mask was empty. Replaced with `ProceduralNoise.Step` (tested).
- **Line endings:** the repo mixes LF and CRLF, and Python text-mode edits on Windows converted whole files. I restored each file's original style; diffs now show only real changes.
- **Truncated file:** one of my own new files was truncated by a cp1252 encoding error during a scripted edit and was rewritten from scratch.
- **Baseline benchmark stall:** one baseline run hung because the release player pauses when unfocused (`runInBackground` is off). The benchmark and showcase runners now force `runInBackground` for command-line runs.
- **Performance regression:** the tread-scroll regression described in Phase 7.
- **Tool behaviour:** PlayMode tests must be run asynchronously through the CLI. `EditorApplication.delayCall` from `eval` never fired.

## Requires independent human playtesting

- **Look and feel:**
  - whether the art direction reads as intended at your monitor and resolution;
  - whether the faction colours are distinguishable for colour-blind players (only colour separates factions);
  - whether the haze, vignette and bloom levels suit you.
- **Readability:**
  - whether effects or damage smoke ever hide units in large battles;
  - whether props near obstacles confuse what is passable.
- **Audio:**
  - the mix and loudness balance, and how repetitive the synthesised sounds become;
  - whether radio-chirp acknowledgements are acceptable instead of voices;
  - whether the engine loop and ambience become tiring.
- **Interface:**
  - main menu and pause flows with real mouse and keyboard;
  - slider feel;
  - the HUD at 1280×720, ultrawide and 4K (only 1920×1080 and 1280×720 benchmark windows were seen).
- **Performance:** on lower-spec hardware, and the isolated stalls.

## Known limitations

- This is coherent, stylised programmer art, not hand-authored production art. There are no UV-unwrapped textures, normal maps or baked AO.
- Factions differ by livery only.
- Animation is procedural only: guns do not elevate, wheels and road wheels do not individually turn, there is no skeletal animation, and treads scroll at one shared speed while moving.
- Audio has no music or voice acting. Positional audio is screen-based, not true 3D.
- The UI still uses Unity's built-in LegacyRuntime font.
- Ambient smoke and dust give way to combat effects when particle budgets fill, so very large fights show less dust.
- Generated meshes are stored as text YAML (about 27 MB). This is deterministic, so rebuilding does not churn git.
- Keyboard shortcuts (F10, and so on) were exercised in PlayMode tests through code, not through real key presses in the built player (the same limitation as M005).
